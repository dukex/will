## Business
- **Problem:** People pick up their phone with intent and get sucked into distraction — notifications, feeds, apps competing for attention instead of enabling action.
- **Solution:** Replaces the Android home screen with a minimal task-based interface: notifications become actionable tasks, the launcher disappears when not needed, and there's no surface area engineered to steal attention.
- **Business model:** Open source / free
- **Defensibility:** Opinionated defaults and open source community trust; the brand itself — being the launcher that openly refuses to manipulate the user is a differentiated position

## Personas

### Primary: Lucas
- **Role:** Developer / product designer
- **Age range:** 25–35
- **Day-in-the-life:** Starts the day with a backlog of Slack messages and GitHub notifications. Uses the phone as a work tool but keeps getting pulled into social media or infinite scroll. Has tried Notion, Todoist, and several launchers — each requires too much maintenance or adds cognitive overhead.
- **Frustration:** Every "minimal" launcher is either all aesthetics with no function, or productivity apps that feel like more work on top of work. He doesn't want to be managed — he wants the phone to stop wasting his time.
- **Aspiration:** Pick up the phone, do the thing, put it down. The phone should match his mental model: there's a task, complete it, move on.
- **Discovery:** GitHub, indie hacker communities, Twitter/X tech circles, word-of-mouth from developer peers.
- **Trust signals:** Trusts open source, clear opinions, no dark patterns. Distrusts apps with engagement metrics, growth hacks, or UI that celebrates streaks.

## Brand Essence

### Emotional Compass
- **brand_heartbeat:** "A marca que recusa participar da economia da atenção — mesmo sendo um app de celular."

### Promise
- **Core promise:** When someone opens Will, they know exactly what they were there to do.
- **Functional promise:** Transforms notification noise into a prioritized task feed; keeps the home screen minimal and intentional.
- **Emotional promise:** Feeling of control — not because the app motivates you, but because it stops interrupting you.

### Point of View
- **Category disagreement:** Will disagrees that productivity apps need to motivate users. Tools don't motivate — they enable. Motivation theater (streaks, badges, check-in prompts) is a distraction wearing a productivity costume.
- **Underestimated truth:** Most phone distraction isn't a willpower problem — it's an environment problem. The home screen is designed to maximize engagement, not enable intent.
- **Manifesto line:** "Your phone should work for you. Not the other way around."

### Personality
- **Personality:** opinionated, precise, no-nonsense
- **Personality reference:** Like Linear meets a Unix tool — obsessive craft, zero tolerance for bloat, opinions you can argue with
- **Not us:** corporate, anxious
- **Never be:** a productivity coach, a motivational app, a launcher that tries to impress
- **Tone:** Direct, dry, builder-to-builder. No cheerleading, no onboarding copy that explains the obvious. Assumes intelligence.

## Competitive Landscape
- **Direct competitors:** Niagara Launcher, Minimalist Phone, Nothing Launcher
- **What sets you apart?** Will is the only launcher that treats notifications as first-class task objects — not just visual noise to manage. It has a point of view, not just an aesthetic.
- **Brands admired:** Linear, Vercel, Raycast — dark, opinionated, builder-friendly, tools that respect the user

## Visual Direction
- **Mood / aesthetic:** Dark, technical, no decoration — terminal energy. Dense but intentional. Every pixel has a reason.
- **Reference links:** linear.app, vercel.com, raycast.com
- **Texture / atmosphere:** Flat dark backgrounds, sharp type, no gradients or drop shadows. Monospace or geometric sans-serif. The UI equivalent of a well-formatted README.
- **Anti-patterns:** No rounded pastel cards, no colorful illustrations, no notification badges disguised as features, no light mode-first design.

## Inspiration
- **Styles liked:** Linear, Vercel, Raycast aesthetic — dark mode first, opinionated whitespace, command-line sensibility
- **Styles to avoid:** Pastel productivity apps, gamified task managers, corporate SaaS with stock photography
- **Existing assets:** ic_launcher only (default Android icon)

## Constraints
- **Timeline:** No hard deadline
- **Budget:** Zero — open source project
- **Must-haves:** Brand must function within Android UI constraints (icons, Material Design baseline)
- **Non-negotiables:** Must work well on Android — iconography and UI must respect platform conventions

## Goals
- **Business goal:** Build a small, loyal community of users who trust the project because it doesn't try to own their attention
- **Brand goal:** Be immediately recognizable as an opinionated tool — not a minimal aesthetic exercise
- **Success metrics:** Community adoption, GitHub stars, developer word-of-mouth
