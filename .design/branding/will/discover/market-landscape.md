# Market Landscape
> Brand: Will | Phase: discover | Date: 2026-04-15

## Android Launcher Market: Size and Trajectory

The Android launcher market is estimated at approximately **$42–200M in 2024–2025** depending on methodology, with projections pointing to contraction at roughly **-7.8% CAGR through 2031** (est. $24M by 2031). The contraction reflects a maturing customization-first segment — not declining interest in home screen alternatives, but consolidation around a smaller number of meaningful products.

Key forces shaping the category right now:

- **Customization demand** remains the top acquisition driver (Nova, Niagara, Hyperion). But pure customization is not a defensible moat — Google continuously absorbs it into Pixel Launcher.
- **AI integration** is the vector most incumbents are betting on: smart folder sorting, predictive app suggestions, context-aware home screens. This is additive complexity bolted onto existing mental models.
- **Privacy and digital wellness** are emerging as genuine differentiators, not just feature bullets. Users are selecting launchers based on what they *don't* do as much as what they do.
- **Open source credibility** is a growing trust signal — especially for developer-adjacent personas. F-Droid category traffic for launchers is growing. Projects like Lawnchair, Neo Launcher, and Kvæsitso (global search, privacy-first) have active communities.

The launcher install base on Android is enormous — Android has roughly **3.9 billion active devices globally** — but the addressable market for *intentional* launcher switchers who stay switched is a small, high-signal fraction of that.

## Attention Economy Backlash

The signal that matters most for Will is not the launcher market specifically. It is the broader shift in how a growing segment of tech workers and designers relate to their phones.

Current data points:

- **1.58 billion people** globally experience some level of smartphone addiction (2025 estimate, up 7.4% YoY).
- U.S. adults check their phones an average of **352 times per day** — once every 2.7 minutes during waking hours.
- Microsoft research puts average attention span at **8 seconds** — shorter than a goldfish. A 2025 study found blocking mobile internet improved sustained attention by the equivalent of reversing 10 years of cognitive aging.
- Slack's own research: employees are interrupted every **11 minutes**, and it takes **23 minutes** to regain deep focus. Cost to U.S. economy: estimated $650 billion annually.
- Focus mode activations on iOS and Android are **up 32% YoY**, concentrated in professionals aged 30–50.
- 82% of users prefer apps that offer dark mode — signaling aesthetic alignment with productivity-first framing.
- The Digital Detox Apps market was $0.4B in 2024, projected to reach **$8.65B by 2035 at 24.3% CAGR**.

Among **developer and designer personas specifically** (Lucas's cohort), the backlash is more ideological than behavioral: they understand attention capture as a design pattern, they resent being subject to it, and they are actively looking for tools built on different principles. This is the group most likely to switch launchers, keep them switched, and evangelize.

Discovery happens through GitHub, indie hacker communities, Twitter/X tech circles, and word-of-mouth from developer peers — exactly where an open source project with a sharp point of view can spread without a marketing budget.

## Digital Wellness: Category Convergence

Three categories are converging in ways that open space for Will:

**1. Digital wellness apps** (Minimalist Phone, One Sec, BeReal's anti-engagement moment) are growing fast but are overlaid on top of the standard home screen — they fight the host environment rather than replace it.

**2. Productivity launchers** (Niagara, Olauncher) reduce noise but don't give notifications meaning — they make tasks implicit at best.

**3. Developer tools** (Linear, Raycast, Notion) have proven that a technical audience will pay premium attention (not money, in open source) to tools that respect their intelligence and don't add cognitive overhead.

Will is the first product that operates at the intersection of all three: it replaces the home screen, makes notifications actionable (not just filterable), and is built with the aesthetic and values of a developer tool. The wellness market validates the problem. The launcher market validates the surface. The developer tools aesthetic validates the tone.
