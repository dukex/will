**Axes:**
- Horizontal: **Wellness-focused** (left) ↔ **Tool-focused** (right)

**Axis explanations:**

- **Nothing Launcher** sits top-center: strong aesthetic identity (the hardware brand DNA), but minimal functional differentiation from stock Android. Not wellness, not tool — pure design statement.
- **Niagara** sits center-left: genuinely wellness-framed messaging ("mental clarity," "digital wellbeing"), with aesthetic polish but limited functional depth around notifications and tasks.
- **Minimalist Phone** sits bottom-left: wellness framing is strongest here (addiction, ADHD, mental health language), but the product is function-heavy with app blockers, schedules, and grayscale mode. More utility than aesthetic.
- **Will** sits bottom-right: the only product that is tool-focused (notifications as tasks, PendingIntent architecture, zero motivational language) and function-driven. Not trying to be calming. Not trying to be beautiful. Trying to work.

**The white space Will owns:** No competitor sits in the bottom-right quadrant. Tool-focused + function-driven with a developer-tool aesthetic is an unclaimed position.

## Competitive Gaps Will Can Claim

**1. Notifications as first-class task objects**
Every competitor either ignores notifications (Nothing), aggregates them (Niagara), or hides them (Minimalist Phone). None converts them into actionable work items. Will is the only launcher with a domain model around notification → task → intent. This is structural differentiation, not a feature flag.

**2. Open source as a trust primitive**
All three major competitors are closed source. For Lucas's cohort — developers who distrust dark patterns and engagement metrics — open source is not a selling point, it is a prerequisite for trust. No competitor has claimed this.

**3. Builder-to-builder tone**
Niagara and Minimalist Phone speak wellness language ("clarity," "wellbeing," "addiction"). Nothing speaks Gen-Z aspirational ("fun again," "built different"). None speak to developers as developers — people who understand that the home screen's engagement mechanics are intentional design decisions, not accidents. Will can own the tone: dry, opinionated, peer-to-peer.

**4. POV as a product feature**
Will has a manifesto, a point of view on the attention economy, and architectural decisions that back it up. Competitors have positioning statements. This is a qualitative gap that creates communities, not just users.

**5. Zero growth mechanics**
No streaks, no usage badges, no engagement rewards. This is invisible as a feature until you've been subjected to the alternative. For Lucas, it registers immediately.
